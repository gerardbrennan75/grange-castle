Kind to your back

Golf is all twisting, bending and carrying. A strong core and lower back act like a natural corset around your spine. EMS builds that support without heavy weights, so it suits people who've been told to go easy at the gym.

Steady on every slope

Uphill lies, downhill lies, wet grass in November. Strong legs, glutes and core keep you balanced through the shot, and help you stay on your feet walking the course.

Strong muscles, strong bones

After the menopause, women lose bone faster as oestrogen drops. When bone gets thinner it's called osteopenia; when it gets thin and fragile enough to break easily, that's osteoporosis. It usually has no symptoms until a fall or a fracture.

Here's the good news: every time a muscle contracts, it pulls on the bone, and that tells the bone to stay strong. Tap the buttons to see the difference.

What the Mayo Clinic says

The Mayo Clinic in the USA uses electrical muscle stimulation in rehabilitation. It says the stimulation "can cause your muscles to contract" and lists these among the possible benefits:

Muscle size & strength
Ability to prevent loss of bone density
Blood circulation & heart health
Overall fitness

Source: Mayo Clinic, "Functional electrical stimulation", mayoclinic.org
